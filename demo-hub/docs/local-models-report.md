# Local models, ContextForge and the instrumented Assistant

*Measured 2026-10-04/05 on the demo stack (`scripts/up.sh`). Every number below comes from the
Assistant's own traces (`demo_hub/telemetry.py`), pantry's per-attempt LLM traces
(`pantry_planner/httptrace.py`), ContextForge's observability API, or the browser's telemetry;
the tables are generated by `python -m demo_hub.report` from the runs of
`scripts/report-bench.sh`. Sample sizes are small (one run per case and configuration): read the
numbers as orders of magnitude and failure modes, not as a leaderboard.*

## Summary

- **Gemini 3 Flash** answered all three cases correctly in a median **5.4 s**, for **$0.0028 per
  answer** on a paid key ($2.80 per 1,000 answers). **Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite** also passed 3/3, but
  was slower here (median 15.1 s: Google held its requests longer) at $0.0016 per answer.
- **Granite 4.2 8B on this laptop's CPU** costs nothing per call and keeps everything on the
  machine, but an answer takes **3.3 minutes** with progressive tool disclosure (2/3 passed; the
  miss was a made-up basket total, caught by the online evals) and **7.7 minutes** with all 14 tools
  offered (3/3 passed; 11.1 minutes as measured, which includes time queued behind a console chat).
- **Progressive disclosure** cut Granite's first prompt from about 6,850 tokens to 1,500-2,040, and
  the prompt tokens per answer from about 14,000 to 4,800: answers came **2.3× faster** (median).
- **Granite's time is all in the model**: reading the prompt (10-15 tokens/s) takes about 57% of an
  answer and writing (1.3-3.6 tokens/s) about 43%. The tools, ContextForge and pantry take under a
  second per answer together. Changes that leave the model alone should bring an answer to about
  2 minutes on this laptop; a smaller model or any GPU is needed for seconds (see Recommendations).
- **ContextForge** adds a median **40-53 ms** per tool call: nothing next to Granite, about 2% of a
  Gemini answer. In return it is one authenticated, scoped endpoint with its own per-call traces.
- **The browser** is fast (largest paint under 170 ms, no long tasks). Its telemetry found three
  problems in the console (a render stall during a turn and two layout shifts), all fixed and
  measured again.

no scope violation). The share of online checks passed is the answer's **confidence**.

The all-tools runs overlapped with a chat from the console between 01:16 and 01:25 (both are in
Ollama's log and in the traces). Ollama answers one request at a time, so each waited for the
other: `tomato-penne-no-us` (all) queued 4.3 minutes, the console chat 3.7. Without the queueing
the all-tools answers took 10.8, 7.7 and 3.1 minutes (median **7.7 min**). Every model step now
records this as **queued** time (its wall time minus Ollama's reading, writing and loading), and
the Metrics page shows its p95 per model. The read rate in the tables is a median over steps, small warm reads included (a
15-token read runs far below the bulk rate); Ollama's log gives 10-15 tokens/s for reads of 250
tokens or more.

**Reasoning.** Granite ran with thinking off in these runs, so its steps carry no reasoning text.
With thinking on (`ollama:granite4.2:8b#think=true`), each step's reasoning (up to 8,000
characters) is kept on its trace span and shown in the Assistant and in the trace view. Gemini
reasons at low effort, but the OpenAI-compatible endpoint the hub uses doesn't return its thoughts.

every line at 0.9, every product an exact match, origins verified for 96% of the spend.

## Where a local model's time goes

### One Granite answer, step by step

The console run of *"Plan tomato penne with nothing from the United States…"* (trace
`tr-4f5932573328`, progressive, 7 min 24 s). Each model step first reads what is new in its prompt,
then writes:

| Step | Reading | Writing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Choose a tool | 2,039 tokens, none cached: **2 min 15 s** | 21 tokens: 6 s |
| 2. Call `plan_recipe` | about 270 new tokens: 20 s | 99 tokens: 39 s |
| 3. Answer | about 1,300 tokens of plan result: **1 min 38 s** | 286 tokens (the Markdown table): **2 min 25 s** |

The tools themselves (`list_recipes`, `plan_recipe` through ContextForge) took 0.1-0.6 s, and pantry's
own planning about 0.2 s.

### Why it is this slow

The rates come from Ollama's own log (`slot print_timing`): reading runs at **10-15 tokens/s**
on this 4-core CPU, writing at **1.3-3.6 tokens/s**. Every token, read or written, runs through all
40 layers of the 8.8B-parameter model. The size of the vocabulary barely matters: its 100,352
entries are about 5% of the weights, so restricting what the model may say would not make it
noticeably faster.

Three things made it slower than it needs to be:

- **Heat.** During the bench, macOS limited the CPU to 63% of its speed (`pmset -g therm`); writing
  slowed from about 3.5 to 2 tokens/s within a single answer.
- **Cache misses.** Ollama keeps the prompt it last read and reuses its unchanged beginning. A warm
  first step read 12-31 new tokens in 2.5-17 s; a cold one read all 2,039 (2 min 15 s above).
  The cache is lost when another conversation or the bench uses the model, or after 30 idle
  minutes. Granite's chat template also puts the tool definitions right after the system prompt,
  so when an observer adds a tool partway through a turn, everything after the system prompt is
  read again (`cheapest-penne`, step 2: 1,406 tokens, 103 s).
- **Queueing.** Ollama runs one request at a time; two chats at once wait for each other (above).

Gemini 3 Flash's steps took 0.9-2.1 s each, except one `recipe-list` step held 29.6 s; Gemini 3.1
Flash-Lite's took 2.5-11.5 s. pantry's HTTP traces (below) show those waits are on Google's side, not
the network's.

## Progressive tool disclosure

The Assistant starts with two tools and `discover_tools`; observers watch the conversation and
add tools when their conditions are met (`demo_hub/assistant_policy.py`).

| Granite 4.2 8B | Progressive | All tools |
|---|---|---|
| Tools at the first step | 3-5 (observers add some from the message) | 14 |
| First prompt | 1,511-2,038 tokens | 6,839-6,855 tokens |
| Prompt tokens per answer (median) | 4,815 | 13,995 |
| A cold first step | 2 min 15 s (2,039 tokens) | 8.3 min (6,097 tokens read in 497 s) |
| Writing speed (median) | 2.9 tokens/s | 1.4 tokens/s |
| Median answer, without queueing | **3.3 min** | **7.7 min** |
| Passed | 2/3 | 3/3 |

With all tools the prompt is 3.5× longer. Once it is cached, reading it again is cheap
(`recipe-list`, run last, took 3.1 minutes either way). But every written token attends over about
7,000 tokens instead of 2,500, so writing is slower (heat played a part: the all-tools runs came
second), and a single cache miss costs over 8 minutes. With all tools Granite also took an extra
step, fetching the recipe before planning it. The progressive miss wasn't a disclosure problem:
Granite called the right tools, then wrote a wrong total.

Disclosure isn't free:

- Each tool an observer adds partway through a turn breaks Ollama's cache (see above).
- The menu clerk's conditions also fired on *"what can I plan?"*, adding `get_recipe`,
  `plan_recipe` and `plan_from_text`, which a listing doesn't need.

On a paid model the all-tools prompt would add about 9,000 input tokens per answer. At Gemini 3
Flash's $0.50 per million that is about $0.0046 more per answer, more than doubling its cost. This
is an estimate from Granite's token counts; Gemini's tokenizer differs.

model per day.

## ContextForge

The Assistant's MCP calls go to ContextForge 1.0.11's `pantry-recipes` virtual server, which forwards
them to pantry's MCP server. That gives one URL and one bearer token, and exposes only the tools that
server is given.

**What it costs.** The hub joins ContextForge's own traces (its `/observability/traces` API) to
each tool call. The gateway overhead is the time ContextForge spent on a call beyond waiting for
pantry: a median **40-53 ms** per call (`get_recipe` 40 ms, `find_product` 46 ms, `list_recipes`
48 ms, `plan_recipe` 53 ms). With 1-3 calls per answer, that is about 2% of a Gemini answer and
nothing next to Granite's minutes.

**What it gives:**

- one authenticated endpoint per audience (a virtual server per toolset);
- a per-call trace of every tool call, which becomes the gateway rows in the Assistant's waterfall;
- performance tracking per tool;
- a timeout that bounds a stuck call.

To enable the traces, set `OBSERVABILITY_ENABLED=true` and `MCPGATEWAY_PERFORMANCE_TRACKING=true`
in ContextForge's `.env`.

**Lessons:**

- **`TOOL_TIMEOUT` must exceed the slowest tool.** At the default 60 s, a store-aware
  `plan_recipe` died while Gemini held pantry's planner for 92 s. The agent then retried without
  the shopping location and answered with made-up stores. It is now 120 s, the agent's
  instructions say to retry with the same location, and the `kept_location` eval flags an answer
  that dropped it.
- **The Admin UI's Observability tab doesn't work in 1.0.11.** Its CSP build of Alpine can't
  evaluate the page's inline component. The API behind it works: the hub reads it, and
  `scripts/gateway-traces.sh` prints recent traces.

mode. Each attempt was timed phase by phase (DNS, connect, TLS, upload, waiting for Google,
download) with Google's own `server-timing` (`docs/data/planner-gemini-calls-2026-10-04.json`):

| Model | Calls | Median | Worst | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini 3 Flash (preview) | 3 | 3.1 s | 3.4 s | Google's own time 2.7-3.3 s of each |
| Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite | 7 | 4.8 s | 48.6 s | one attempt held **43.5 s** by Google, then HTTP 503; another 13.3 s |

In every traced call the network took under 0.1 s (DNS 0.00-0.02 s, connect, TLS 0.02 s, upload
0.05 s); the wait was inside Google, which its `server-timing` confirmed to within 0.1 s. The day's

The Assistant once lost a whole store-aware plan to this: a 92 s hold passed ContextForge's 60 s
tool timeout, and the agent retried without the shopping location.

## The frontend, measured

The console reports its own performance to the hub (`src/telemetry.ts`):

- per page load: largest paint, layout shift and which elements moved, the slowest interaction,
  and long tasks;
- every API call, with the hub's own time from `Server-Timing`;
- per Assistant turn: time to first byte and to the first event, how late events arrived, and how
  long the page took to paint them.

It found three problems:

| Problem | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Assistant: the whole chat re-rendered every second while a turn ran (the progress clock) | render p95 920 ms, slowest interaction 2.44 s | render p95 9 ms, slowest interaction 96 ms |
| Assistant: the model pickers resized as their options loaded | layout shift 0.30-0.31 | 0.002-0.012 |
| Metrics: sections filled in one at a time | layout shift 0.22 | 0.010 |

Two measurement bugs were also fixed:

- The slowest interaction had counted pointer moves while the page was busy.
- A page reported its numbers only once per load, so a slow click late in a session was missed.

Everything else is healthy:

- Pages: largest paint 124-168 ms, first byte p50 20 ms, no long tasks.
- API calls: the hub's own routes p50 3-7 ms (`Server-Timing`); calls proxied to pantry 18-57 ms.
- Chat stream: first byte 62 ms, first event 272 ms, event lag p95 35 ms.

The header polls pantry's health and runtime settings: 149 calls each over this session, about
32 ms apiece. That is cheap, but it is most of the console's traffic.

## Recommendations

1. **For an interactive demo, use Gemini 3 Flash.** It passed every case in about 5 s, for under
   $3 per 1,000 answers. Keep Granite for offline, private or keyless use, and as the fallback.
2. **Keep progressive disclosure for local models**, and keep each turn's toolset stable:
   - decide the tools from the user's message before the first step;
   - or append tools at the end of the list, so Ollama's cache survives.

   Tighten the menu clerk so a listing request adds no planning tools.
3. **Make Granite faster without changing the model.** Estimated together: 7.4 minutes → about 2
   minutes per answer on this laptop.
   - Keep the instructions and tool definitions warm: a warm-up request at start-up and after each
     turn, plus a second Ollama slot so the bench and the console don't evict each other.
   - Let code render the plan table from the tool result, and have the model write two or three
     sentences. That saves about 2 minutes of writing, and a made-up total becomes impossible.
   - Send local models a compact text plan instead of JSON: about 1,300 tokens become about 400.
4. **Then measure smaller and faster setups** with the same bench:
   - Granite 4's mixture-of-experts H-Tiny (7B total, about 1B active per token) and its 3B Micro;
   - speculative decoding (a small draft model, or n-grams copied from the prompt) via llama.cpp's
     server;
   - a Q4_0 build that can use this CPU's AVX-512 kernels.

   Only if the small models fail the bench, fine-tune one on these traces; that needs a GPU for
   the training. Keep the laptop cool and plugged in during runs. Any Apple Silicon Mac or recent
   NVIDIA GPU would make Granite roughly 5-10× faster at writing and over 10× at reading.
5. **Keep ContextForge's tool timeout above the slowest tool call**, and keep the hub's join of its
   traces while the Admin UI's Observability tab is broken.
6. **Keep the online evals on every answer.** `grounded_money` caught the one wrong answer here, and
   `kept_location` guards against the lost-store failure.
7. **Run more repetitions before comparing pass rates.** One run per case shows failure modes, not
   reliability.

## Reproduce

```bash
cd pantry-platform/demo-hub
scripts/up.sh                                   # the stack, pantry in demo mode: START_DEMO_MODE=1
scripts/report-bench.sh ~/.pantry-demo/bench/report        # Granite: progressive and all tools
SKIP_LOCAL=1 WITH_GEMINI=1 scripts/report-bench.sh ~/.pantry-demo/bench/report   # Gemini

```

(model steps, tool calls, ContextForge's spans, pantry's steps) and its evals.
